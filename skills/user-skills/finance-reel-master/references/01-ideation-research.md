# Stage 1: Ideation & Research

This stage produces two things the rest of the pipeline depends on:

1. An **Idea Brief**: the chosen idea, its one-line promise, the angle, and hook seeds.
2. A **Research Pack**: every number with a primary source, date and unit, verified.

A script written without these tends to sound confident and contain errors. For a finance brand, one wrong number costs more trust than ten good reels earn, so this stage is where accuracy gets locked in.

## Core principles

- **One reel, one idea, one hero number or question.** If an idea needs two hero numbers, it is two reels or a series.
- **Ideas must be sendable.** Ask: who would someone DM this to? Instagram's top-three ranking signals are watch time, likes per reach and sends per reach. Sends matter most for reaching non-followers, so useful, surprising, "you need to see this" ideas travel furthest.
- **Numbers come from primary sources.** Never invent a figure, estimate silently, or rely on remembered numbers. If a figure can't be verified, flag it or cut it.
- **Rebuild, don't repost.** Instagram demotes reposted or aggregated content. News and other people's charts are inputs to research, never the visual itself.

## Step 1.1: Generate candidates (default: 10)

If web search is available, first check what is in the business news this week, and prefer topics with a live hook. Then pull ideas from these sources:

| Source | Where to look | Typical angle |
|---|---|---|
| News calendar | Central bank policy announcements, inflation releases, budget speech, results season, IMF/rating news, fuel/electricity price revisions, trade and tourism data | "X just happened. Here's what it means for you." |
| "Why does X do Y?" | Everyday business puzzles | Counter-intuitive business model |
| Company stories | Annual reports, histories, founders, collapses | Rise, crisis, turn, today |
| Money myths | Common beliefs about FDs, gold, land, leasing, credit cards | Myth, evidence, truth, what to do |
| Shocking numbers | Debt, scale, market cap, per-capita figures | Number, scale comparison, cause, so what |
| Comparisons | Then vs now, A vs B, Sri Lanka vs peers | Split-screen reveal |
| Hidden systems | Remittances, card fees, pricing, supply chains | "How X actually works" |
| Audience questions | Comments, DMs, search autocomplete | Direct answer |
| Competitor outliers | Reels beating their own account average by 3× or more | Same question, better answer, your angle |

**Sri Lanka calendar hooks (default market):**
- Central Bank of Sri Lanka policy rate decisions.
- Department of Census & Statistics CCPI/NCPI inflation releases.
- Annual budget proposals (tax, excise, price changes).
- Colombo Stock Exchange (CSE) quarterly results and dividends.
- IMF programme reviews and credit-rating actions.
- Monthly tourism arrivals, worker remittances and export earnings.
- Fuel and electricity price revisions.
- Global events with a local angle: oil prices, US rates, tariffs, India.

## Step 1.2: Shape each idea into an angle

| Archetype | Psychological driver | Hook seed template |
|---|---|---|
| Why question | Curiosity gap, surprise | "Why does {familiar thing} {counter-intuitive behaviour}?" |
| Shock number | Awe | "{Entity} {does thing} worth {number}." |
| Myth-buster | Anxiety + practical utility | "{Common belief} is costing you {X}." |
| Stakes / you | Personal relevance | "If you have a {loan/FD/salary}, {event} just changed {thing}." |
| Company story | Narrative, awe | "In {year}, {company} was worth {small}. Today, {big}." |
| Comparison | Social currency | "{A} vs {B}. One of them {surprising}." |
| Hidden system | Insider knowledge | "Here's where your {money/thing} actually goes." |
| Reveal | Curiosity | "The {superlative} in {market} isn't who you think." |

Research on online sharing (Berger & Milkman) finds that high-arousal emotions (awe, surprise, anxiety, anger) and practical usefulness increase sharing, while low-arousal sadness reduces it. Aim every angle at one of these.

## Step 1.3: Score and choose

Score each idea 1–5 on seven criteria. Keep ideas scoring **24/35 or more**. Break ties on timeliness, then visualisability.

| Criterion | 1 = weak | 5 = strong |
|---|---|---|
| Curiosity gap | Answer is obvious | Viewer must watch to find out |
| Surprise / awe | Known fact | "Wait, really?" |
| Practical utility | Trivia | Changes a money decision |
| Sendability | No one to send it to | Obvious person to DM |
| Visualisability | Talking points only | One clear chart or image carries it |
| Timeliness | Evergreen, no hook | Live news hook this week |
| Source availability | No primary data | Clean official data exists |

Present the shortlist as a table: idea, archetype, score breakdown, total, one-line reason.

## Step 1.5: Build the Research Pack

**Source tiers.** Use Tier 1 for every number that appears on screen or in the VO.

- **Tier 1 (numbers):**
  - Central bank publications: CBSL annual report, press releases, statistical tables.
  - National statistics offices: DCS.
  - Ministry of Finance and budget documents.
  - Stock exchange filings and company annual reports.
  - IMF and World Bank data.
  - Regulator releases.
- **Tier 2 (context, quotes, timelines only):** reputable business press, such as Echelon, Daily FT, EconomyNext, Reuters, Bloomberg and the FT. When a press story cites a number, trace it back to the Tier 1 original.
- **Tier 3 (never a source):** social posts, blogs, wikis, forums, other reels, AI-generated text, and figures remembered without a source.

**Research Pack template:**

| # | Claim (as it will be said) | Number | Unit / currency | Period / date | Source (doc + page/table + URL) | Tier | Verified by | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

**Number hygiene:**
- **Labels:** always record unit, currency, nominal vs real, and the period. Distinguish "as at" figures from "for the year" figures.
- **Currency:** use the latest release, and note the release date. For LKR↔USD conversions, record the rate and its date. Sri Lankan house style: "Rs." in VO and captions, "LKR" in charts and formal labels (adjust to the brand's style guide).
- **Derived numbers:** show the working for per-capita figures, growth rates and "X times bigger" claims, and recompute them yourself.
- **Rounding:** round for speech ("nearly two trillion rupees") but keep the exact figure in the pack and in the on-screen source line.
- **Estimates:** label them "est." and say whose estimate it is.
- **Charts:** bar charts start at zero, and comparisons use the same scale. Misleading axes destroy finance credibility.

**If web search is available:** search for the primary document and fetch it rather than relying on snippets, then cite the URL.

**If web search is not available:** fill numbers as `{TO VERIFY: what, where to find it}` and never guess.

## Step 1.6: Fact-check gate (must pass before scripting)

1. Trace every claim to a Tier 1 source, or rewrite it as clearly attributed opinion or context.
2. Recompute all derived numbers.
3. Cross-check key figures with a second source where one exists.
4. Confirm each figure is the latest release.
5. Check for **sensitivities**:
   - Named companies or people in a negative light: stick to documented facts and attribute them.
   - No buy/sell/hold calls on specific securities.
   - Investing topics get an "educational, not financial advice" line.
   - Present politically contested topics with balance.
   - Avoid speculation that could move a share price.
6. Produce three lists: **Verified**, **Removed/unverifiable**, and **Needs human check**. A second person signs off on the hero number.

## Handoff

Pass the Idea Brief and the verified Research Pack to Stage 2 (`finance-reel-02-script`). If that skill isn't installed, write the script hook-first: a 3-variant hook, open loop, rehook every 8–12 s, payoff, then a send/save CTA.
